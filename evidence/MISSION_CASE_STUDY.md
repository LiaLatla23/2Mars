# Mission walkthrough · 2Mars V4

This document is a **reproducible observation guide**, not a report of a specific recorded playthrough. It describes what to do in the current game and what evidence to capture while playing. Do not treat the checklist as proof that a particular outcome has already been achieved.

## Suggested walkthrough

| Step | Player action | What to observe and record |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Briefing | Start a new Campaign mission and review the briefing. | Mission objective, profile choices and constraints presented to the player. |
| 2. Planning | Select a mission profile, launch window, landing site and science priority. | Which choices are available and how the planning screen explains them. |
| 3. Rover design | Configure a rover using the workshop. | Selected chassis, wheels, power system, computer, arm and instruments; resulting mass, cost, power and compatibility checks. |
| 4. Readiness | Review the readiness assessment before launch. | Blockers, limitations, risks and recommendations. Record any design change made in response. |
| 5. Launch and transit | Complete the launch and transit stages. | Sequence, decisions and the way the game communicates the simplified mission timeline. |
| 6. Mars operations | Drive to targets, investigate, use instruments, collect samples and manage data. | Which instruments are used, what evidence is collected, operational constraints and any failed or incomplete action. |
| 7. Communications | Store and transmit collected data. | Difference between collecting observations and successfully returning data. |
| 8. Debrief | Complete required objectives and review the debrief/progress screens. | Final mission status, science results, inventory and recommendations shown by the game. |

## Comparing two rover designs

A strong demonstration can compare two real builds for the same mission objective:

- **Build A:** a lower-cost or lower-mass configuration.
- **Build B:** a configuration that prioritizes a different capability, such as power, mobility, storage or science instruments.

Record the actual values displayed by V4 and the resulting readiness assessment and mission outcomes. Do not invent values or assume that one design is universally superior; the purpose is to show the trade-offs.

## Interpretation

Use the in-game Science Library and the [NASA resource inventory](../research/NASA_RESOURCES_USED.md) to distinguish real mission facts from fictional samples, game parameters and simplified mechanics. A game result demonstrates how the model behaves; it is not a prediction of how a real NASA mission would perform.
