# Mission walkthrough · 2Mars V5.1

This is a reproducible observation guide for inspecting the current game. It is not a report of a particular recorded playthrough and does not claim a specific mission result.

## Suggested walkthrough

| Step | Player action | What to observe |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Briefing | Start a new mission and review Dr. Owen's briefing. | Mission goals, profile choices and constraints. |
| 2. Planning | Select a mission profile, launch scenario, landing area and science priority. | Available choices and how the game explains them. |
| 3. Rover design | Configure the rover in the workshop. | Selected components and resulting mass, cost, power, mobility and compatibility. |
| 4. Readiness | Review the readiness assessment. | Blockers, risks, limitations and recommendations. |
| 5. Launch and transit | Complete launch and transit stages. | Sequence, mission events and simplified timeline. |
| 6. Mars operations | Drive to targets, photograph and analyse rocks, use instruments, collect samples and manage resources. | Instrument use, evidence gathered, constraints and unsuccessful actions. |
| 7. Communications | Store and transmit observations. | Difference between collecting information and returning data. |
| 8. Debrief | Complete mission objectives and review results. | Mission status, scientific results, inventory and progression. |
| 9. Optional co-op | Open local cooperative or Expedition mode. | Role coordination or shared mission state across two rovers. |

## Comparing rover designs

A useful demonstration can compare two configurations for the same mission objective:

- **Configuration A:** prioritize lower mass or lower cost.
- **Configuration B:** prioritize another capability, such as power, mobility, storage or science instruments.

Record the values shown by the game and the resulting readiness assessment and mission outcome. Do not invent values or assume one design is universally superior; the purpose is to observe trade-offs.

## Interpretation

Use the in-game Science Library and [NASA resource inventory](../research/NASA_RESOURCES_USED.md) to distinguish real mission facts from fictional samples, game parameters and simplified mechanics. A game result demonstrates how the educational model behaves; it is not a prediction of how a real NASA mission would perform.
